The basics

What a mutual fund is

Many investors pool money; a professional manager buys shares, bonds or both with it. You own units, and the unit price (NAV) moves with the value of what the fund holds.

A SEBI-regulated trust. NAV is struck daily at market value; costs are taken through the total expense ratio (TER), already netted in the NAV.

Direct vs regular plans

The same fund comes in two versions. The direct plan has no distributor commission, so it costs less and usually returns more over time.

The TER gap is typically 0.5–1.5 pts a year in equity funds; compounded over 15–20 years it is a large share of final wealth. Compare direct-plan TERs.

The three big risks

Market risk: prices fall. Interest-rate risk: bond prices fall when rates rise. Credit risk: a borrower fails to repay.

Equity: volatility and drawdown. Debt: duration (rate sensitivity) and credit quality. Check the SEBI riskometer and the potential risk class (PRC) matrix for debt funds.

Debt funds

For stability and income: an alternative to fixed deposits. Two dials matter: how long the bonds are (rate risk) and who borrowed (credit risk).

Rule of thumb: a fund with a modified duration of 5 years loses about 5% if interest rates rise by 1 percentage point, and gains about 5% if they fall by 1 point. Short-duration funds barely move; long-duration and gilt funds move a lot.

Choosing a fund: a simple checklist

  1. Start from the goal and the time horizon. Money needed within 1 year: liquid or money-market. 1–3 years: short-duration or arbitrage. Over 5–7 years: mainly equity.
  2. Decide the asset mix first, the fund second. The split between equity, debt and gold explains most of your result.
  3. Prefer direct plans and low costs, especially for index and large-cap exposure.
  4. Keep it small: 3–5 funds are usually enough. More funds often means the same stocks twice.
  1. Judge performance on rolling returns over 3 and 5 years against the right benchmark (total-return index), not on last year's ranking.
  2. For debt funds, read the factsheet: modified duration, yield to maturity and credit ratings tell you the risk.
  3. Use SIPs and rebalance yearly; move more into equity when it is cheap and less when it is expensive.
  4. Check tax and exit loads before switching: rules change, so confirm the current treatment.
